BLUE WAVE SWEEPS MIDTERMS: DEMOCRATS SEIZE CONGRESS, FLIP KEY GOVERNORSHIPS

Democrats are projected to win both the Senate and the House in the 3 November midterms, and flipped governor's mansions in Texas, Georgia, Ohio and Iowa.

4 November 2026, 09:13 · Source: NBC News, CBS News, Associated Press

WASHINGTON — Democrats are projected to win control of both chambers of the US Congress in Tuesday's midterm elections, according to NBC News and CBS News, ending Republican control of the House and Senate. The party also flipped governorships in several states long held by Republicans, including Texas, Georgia, Ohio and Iowa.

In the Senate, Democrats needed a net gain of four seats. CBS News projected they reached that mark, with upsets in Texas, South Carolina and Kansas, plus pickups in Ohio and North Carolina, along with a flip in Iowa. CNBC reported that Democrats are projected to hold at least 51 seats, the minimum needed for control because Vice President JD Vance breaks ties.

In Iowa, Democrat Josh Turek defeated Republican Rep. Ashley Hinson for the open seat. In South Carolina, Democrat Annie Andrews defeated Republican Sen. Darline Graham, who was appointed to the seat this summer after the death of her brother, Sen. Lindsey Graham, and the Associated Press called the race for Andrews. Other projected flips include James Talarico over Ken Paxton in Texas, Roy Cooper over Michael Whatley in North Carolina, Sherrod Brown over Sen. Jon Husted in Ohio, and Adam Hamilton over Sen. Roger Marshall in Kansas. Democratic Sen. Jon Ossoff held his seat in Georgia.

In the House, ABC News projected early on Wednesday that Democrats had won enough seats to take control of the chamber. Final tallies in both chambers may still change as remaining races are counted.

The governor's races produced several landmark results:

Texas: Democrat Gina Hinojosa was projected to defeat incumbent Republican Gov. Greg Abbott after Abbott conceded, making her the state's first Democratic governor in decades.

Georgia: Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms defeated Republican Rick Jackson, the AP projected, to become the first Black woman elected governor of Georgia.

Ohio: Democrat Amy Acton defeated Republican Vivek Ramaswamy, the AP projected, and is reported to be the first woman elected governor of Ohio.

Iowa: Democrat Rob Sand won the open governor's seat, the AP reported, flipping a seat Republicans have held for more than two decades.

The results are a significant setback for President Donald Trump, who has said he will accept the outcome. Democrats will take control of Congress in January, giving them the power to set the legislative agenda and lead committee oversight for the remaining two years of the presidential term.
