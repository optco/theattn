Columbia Engineers Develop Implantable Smart Stent to Detect and Electrocute Circulating Cancer Cells

Columbia biomedical engineers have unveiled the Stentinel, an implantable device that catches and destroys circulating tumor cells in the bloodstream before they can seed secondary tumors.

30 October 2026, 12:22 · Source: Web

NEW YORK — Biomedical engineers at Columbia University have unveiled a groundbreaking implantable medical device designed to act as a permanent internal filter, catching and instantly destroying circulating tumor cells (CTCs) before they can form deadly secondary tumors.

The device, named The Stentinel, represents a radical shift in oncological research, shifting the focus from treating large, established tumors to neutralizing the early cellular seeds of metastasis directly within the bloodstream.

An Electronic Filter in the Veins

Developed by acclaimed oncologist Dr. Azra Raza alongside Columbia biomedical engineers Samia and Ken Shepard, the Stentinel is built to address the deadliest phase of cancer: metastasis. When a primary tumor sheds cells into the blood, these CTCs travel to other organs, creating new, often untreatable growths.

The Stentinel is designed to intercept them at this exact stage.

The device resembles a standard coronary stent but is integrated with advanced electronics, a computer chip, and micro-electrodes. Using a minimally invasive procedure, it is folded up, loaded into a specialized syringe mechanism, and injected directly into a major vein—such as the subclavian vein or a vein in the arm—where it expands and anchors permanently into place.

The Mechanism: Sense, Flag, and Zap

Once deployed, the Stentinel operates continuously, using a sophisticated electronic detection system based on electrical impedance:

  • The Scan: The chip generates a localized, harmless electrical field within the vein, measuring the electrical resistance of passing blood cells.
  • The ID: Circulating tumor cells are significantly larger than normal red and white blood cells. As they flow through the stent, their physical size disrupts the electrical field in a distinct, measurable pattern.
  • The Strike: The onboard computer instantly identifies this cellular signature and triggers a localized, high-voltage electrical pulse, effectively electrocuting the cancer cell before it can escape the bloodstream.

Because the pulse is highly targeted and brief, it destroys the larger cancer cells without damaging smaller, healthy blood cells or the surrounding blood vessel wall.

A New Frontier in Electrical Oncology

While therapies like NanoKnife (Irreversible Electroporation) already use needle electrodes to zap localized solid tumors in organs like the liver or pancreas, the Stentinel is the first technology aimed at hunting moving cancer cells in real time.

Currently moving through preclinical testing and development phases, the research marks a major milestone in bioelectronic medicine. If successful in human clinical trials, the device could offer high-risk cancer patients a permanent, automated line of defense against the spread of disease, redefining what it means to intercept cancer at its very inception.
