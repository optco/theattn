Scripps study explores biweekly GLP-1 dosing for weight loss maintenance

Early data from Scripps Clinic suggests some patients may keep their weight off after stretching GLP-1 injections from weekly to every two weeks, but experts warn the study is small and uncontrolled.

29 October 2026, 22:33 · Source: Scripps Clinic

SAN DIEGO — Early real-world data from Scripps Clinic suggests that extending the dosing schedule of popular GLP-1 weight-loss medications from weekly to every two weeks may allow some patients to maintain their weight loss, body composition and metabolic improvements over a 36-week period.

While the preliminary findings have generated interest among obesity medicine specialists, experts emphasize that significant caveats remain regarding the study's design, scale and broad applicability.

Early findings offer potential cost and supply relief

The observational data evaluates patients who moved from standard seven-day dosing intervals to a 14-day schedule during their weight-maintenance phase.

For clinicians and patients facing high out-of-pocket costs, supply shortages or persistent mild side effects, the prospect of lengthening the dosing interval is an attractive alternative. Spacing out injections could lower monthly treatment costs and reduce overall medication demand while preserving clinical outcomes for select individuals.

Experts urge caution over study limitations

Despite the encouraging headline, medical researchers note that the study's structure limits how broadly its results can be applied.

  • Small cohort size: The findings are based on a small retrospective review of about 30 patients.
  • Selection bias: Retrospective observational reviews tend to select for patients who are already doing well on treatment. Those chosen to move to a biweekly schedule may have been more responsive to GLP-1 therapy or more consistent with lifestyle changes.
  • No control group: Because the study was not a randomized controlled trial (RCT), it cannot show that biweekly dosing works as well as weekly dosing across a general patient population.

Pharmacokinetics vs. rebound risks

Pharmacological models show that stretching injection intervals to 14 days leads to lower trough concentrations of GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide, in the bloodstream.

While some patients maintain their metabolic improvements at lower trough levels, reduced exposure often leads to weaker appetite suppression and lower overall efficacy over time. Major randomized maintenance trials, such as STEP-4 and SURMOUNT-4, have consistently shown that continuous, robust GLP-1 exposure is typically required to protect against metabolic rebound and weight regain.

Current clinical impact

Extending dosing intervals remains an off-label practice. While obesity medicine physicians are increasingly exploring de-escalation strategies tailored to individual patients, researchers stress that large randomized trials are needed before biweekly GLP-1 maintenance can be considered a standard treatment protocol.
