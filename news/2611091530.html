Shadows of Division: How Israel’s Separation Wall Compares to the Berlin Wall

On the anniversary of the Berlin Wall’s fall, a look at how the West Bank barrier compares in length, height and purpose, and where the comparison breaks down.

9 November 2026, 12:00 · Source: Al Jazeera, Associated Press, Israel Institute

Thirty-seven years after crowds began pulling down the Berlin Wall on 9 November 1989, the barrier Israel has built in and around the occupied West Bank is again being set beside it. The comparison is vivid, widely used and heavily contested.

By the numbers, the Israeli barrier is the larger structure. Its planned route is about 708 kilometres, more than double the length of the Green Line, the 1949 armistice line it loosely follows. The Berlin Wall, which encircled West Berlin, ran roughly 155 kilometres.

Height is a different story. In urban areas such as Jerusalem and Bethlehem the barrier is a concrete wall of about nine metres, more than twice the height of Berlin’s. But concrete makes up only a small share of the route. Most of it is a fence with sensors, patrol roads and trenches, backed by an exclusion zone that in places reaches 100 metres.

The route is central to the dispute. Around 85 per cent of the barrier runs inside the West Bank rather than along the Green Line, cutting some farmers off from their land and separating families from nearby towns. Palestinians also describe a system of checkpoints and permits that governs daily movement.

Israel says the barrier is a security measure, built in 2002 at the height of the Second Intifada. The Associated Press has reported that suicide bombings in Israel fell from 41 in 2002 to five in 2005. Researchers disagree on how much of that fall the barrier explains. Other factors cited include improved intelligence, military operations and ceasefires. A Hebrew University study concluded that the barrier itself contributed to the drop in attacks.

The legal position is also clear-cut on one side. In 2004 the International Court of Justice issued an advisory opinion that the construction of the wall in occupied Palestinian territory was contrary to international law. Israel rejects that view and argues the barrier is a lawful, temporary security measure.

The most basic difference is purpose. The Berlin Wall was built by East Germany to stop its own citizens leaving. The West Bank barrier is intended by Israel to keep people out of Israeli towns, though critics say its route and the restrictions that come with it amount to separation and control of Palestinians.

Another contrast is how each structure might end. The Berlin Wall came down with a political collapse. Israeli officials tie any removal of the barrier to an end of violence, while Palestinians argue it should follow an end to the occupation.
