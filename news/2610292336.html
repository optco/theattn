Alphabet suffers worst day ever as AI spending turns cash flow negative

Shares in Google's parent fell nearly 13%, wiping out about $500 billion in value, after a third-quarter miss on earnings, weaker search growth and a $9.07 billion cash outflow.

29 October 2026, 23:36 · Source: CNBC, Washington Times, RTÉ

MOUNTAIN VIEW, California — Alphabet shares fell nearly 13% on Thursday, their worst day on record, after a third-quarter earnings report showed weaker-than-expected search growth and a swing to negative free cash flow as the company spends heavily on artificial intelligence. The drop erased around $500 billion of market value and passed the previous record one-day fall of 11.63% set in March 2020, according to CNBC.

Alphabet reported its results after the market closed on Wednesday. Revenue rose 25% to $127.58 billion, but came in below analysts' estimates of about $128 billion. Earnings were $2.12 per share. Excluding an $8.7 billion loss on equity investments, which the company said cut earnings by 55 cents a share, adjusted earnings were $2.67, against the $3.02 analysts expected. The investment losses were linked to Alphabet's stakes in private companies including SpaceX.

AI Mode puts pressure on search

Search and other revenue grew 12% to about $63.4 billion, down from 17% growth in the second quarter. Chief business officer Philipp Schindler said the slowdown in search advertising was bigger than expected, partly because of greater use of AI Mode, Google's chatbot-style search interface. Ads in AI Mode currently earn less than traditional search results, and Google is still experimenting with advertising in the product.

Investors have long worried that AI answers could eat into the search business that makes most of Alphabet's money. Dave Wagner of Aptus Capital Advisors said the market wants to see search accelerating, not slowing, and that until AI proves to add to search revenue the shares are likely to trade lower.

A $51 billion spending bill

Capital spending reached $51.36 billion in the quarter, more than double the $23.95 billion a year earlier and higher than the company's operating cash flow of $42.29 billion. Free cash flow was negative $9.07 billion, much worse than the roughly negative $2.7 billion analysts expected and the second negative quarter in a row, after negative $5.9 billion in the second quarter. A year earlier it was positive $24.46 billion.

Finance chief Anat Ashkenazi kept Alphabet's 2026 capital spending forecast at $195 billion to $205 billion. She said spending would rise significantly in 2027, with more details to come on the fourth-quarter call, and warned that building up its stock of Google's own AI chips would keep pressure on free cash flow.

Cloud is the bright spot

Google Cloud was the strongest part of the report. Its revenue more than doubled to $30.71 billion and beat estimates. That was not enough to calm investors, who are watching closely what big technology companies get back from their AI spending. Microsoft, which also reported this week, traded largely flat on Thursday.

CEO Sundar Pichai called it a strong quarter for the company.
