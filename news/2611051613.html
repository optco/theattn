Canva Announces Major Restructure Affecting Nearly 1,300 Roles Amid Tech Squeeze

Australian design software giant Canva restructures its workforce, cutting roughly 22% of roles to flatten management and manage rising compute costs associated with AI capabilities.

5 November 2026, 16:12 · Source: 9News

SYDNEY — Australian design software giant Canva has announced the first major restructuring in its 13-year history, affecting approximately 1,271 employees—roughly 22% of its global workforce.

In a memo sent to staff by co-founder and Chief Executive Officer Melanie Perkins, the company confirmed plans to flatten internal management structures, consolidate product teams, and adjust office location policies. Perkins described the decision as "without doubt the hardest moment in our thirteen-year history."

Reorganization and AI Economics

The announcement arrives as Canva navigates broader economic pressures in the tech sector, marked by slowing growth and rising operational costs associated with compute-heavy Artificial Intelligence features.

While external industry analysis has highlighted the financial squeeze AI tools place on software subscriptions, Canva's internal memo pointed to operational evolution and changing customer expectations rather than direct replacement of personnel by AI algorithms. The reorganization aims to streamline decision-making by merging product, growth, and platform divisions into a single unified organization.

The total of 1,271 affected positions spans departures, role changes, and geographic relocations. As part of the structural shift, Sydney will become the primary base for several product teams, and the company is rolling back aspects of its remote-first policy by requiring hybrid staff to work in-office two to three days a week starting in January.

Severance Package and Entrepreneurship Grant

For staff facing redundancy, Canva announced a severance framework offering a minimum of six months’ salary (inclusive of statutory entitlements), extended health benefits, acceleration of equity vesting, and career transition support.

Additionally, the company is introducing a US$10,000 grant program for departing team members who register interest in pursuing independent projects, startups, or community initiatives.

The restructuring reflects an ongoing trend across the global tech landscape, where major software firms are realigning budgets and team structures to balance compute infrastructure investments with long-term profitability goals.
