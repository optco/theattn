Lula wins fourth term in Brazil after comeback runoff victory

Brazil's president beat Flavio Bolsonaro by more than four million votes, but a challenge to the result and a hostile Congress lie ahead.

26 October 2026, 11:20 · Source: Reuters, via TimesLIVE

Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva has won a fourth term as president of Brazil, the electoral court announced after Sunday's runoff. He defeated Senator Flavio Bolsonaro, the eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, by a margin of more than four million votes.

The result defied the polls, which had the race close, and cut against a run of right-wing victories elsewhere in Latin America. Bolsonaro had finished first in the opening round three weeks earlier, with 47% to Lula's 45%. Lula is the first candidate since Brazil's return to democracy in the 1980s to win the presidency after finishing second in the first round.

Bolsonaro said he would ask for the result to be annulled, pointing to a Sunday supreme court decision that extended voting hours by two hours. Analysts told Reuters that an annulment is unlikely given the size of his defeat, which was more than double the margin by which his father lost to Lula in 2022. Mario Braga of the risk consultancy Rane said protests by right-wing supporters were possible in the coming days but a reversal of the outcome was very improbable.

Jair Bolsonaro never accepted his 2022 defeat and was later sentenced to 27 years in prison for plotting a coup to stay in power.

Lula, who is 80, served as president from 2003 to 2011. He was jailed from 2018 to 2019 on corruption charges that were later overturned, which cleared the way for his return to power in 2022. His campaign this year faced voter frustration over corruption scandals and rising prices. Pollsters said stronger than expected turnout helped him in the runoff.

Late in the campaign Lula promised free public transport on weekends nationwide, which still has to be formally put to Congress, and a 15% increase in his flagship welfare programme. He also pointed to falling poverty and inequality and record lows in unemployment and deforestation during his last term, and warned that Bolsonaro would cut welfare. Lula also accused Bolsonaro of being ready to hand Brazil's sovereignty to US President Donald Trump, whose administration had backed the Bolsonaros' return to power.

The win deprives Washington of another friendly right-wing government in the region. But Lula's new term could be his hardest. Brazil has elected its most conservative Congress since the 1980s, and Bolsonaro's Liberal Party and its allies could hold more than 280 seats in the lower house and up to 50 in the Senate, enough for comfortable majorities against the government.

The economy is also a worry. The government expects growth of just 2.3% next year, while public debt has risen above 82% of GDP, the highest since the pandemic. Goldman Sachs expects Lula to make only modest progress on containing spending, and analysts say a right-leaning Congress is likely to push business-friendly reforms and spending cuts that could provoke public backlash.
