Blood on the Streets: Over $2 Trillion Wiped Out in One Day as Post-Election Panic Grips Wall Street

The Nasdaq crashed 5.52% and the S&P 500 fell 4.36% in their worst day since April 2025, as fears of an AI crackdown under a Democratic Congress triggered a global sell-off.

8 November 2026, 12:00 · Source: Reuters, AP, Fox Business

NEW YORK — In a historic, stomach-churning collapse, the U.S. stock market erased more than $2 trillion in wealth in a single session on Thursday, as post-midterm election panic set off a frenzy of algorithmic and human selling that stunned even veteran traders.

What began as a sigh of relief on Wednesday, when the Dow surged more than 1,500 points, turned into a bloodbath 24 hours later. Investors woke up to a harsh new reality: a dramatic shift in congressional power and a wave of state-level Democratic victories that Wall Street fears could derail the artificial intelligence revolution.

The great tech massacre

The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite bore the brunt, plummeting 5.52% to close at 24,315 — its worst session since the tariff rout of April 2025. The S&P 500 plunged 4.36% to about 7,354, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 1,833 points, or roughly 3.4%, wiping out Wednesday’s entire rally. Every stock in the Dow fell.

The Nasdaq 100 sank 6.36%, and the Nasdaq officially entered correction territory, more than 10% below its October record high. Some reports put the value erased from Nasdaq-listed companies alone at around $2.5 trillion.

Mega-cap tech titans were hammered as a tidal wave of sell orders overwhelmed buyers. Tesla tumbled more than 10%, Nvidia lost over 9%, and chipmaker AMD collapsed by more than 17%. Meta and Alphabet also saw their market caps vaporised within hours.

AI under siege

The catalyst for the panic was a sudden realisation that the golden goose of the modern bull market — artificial intelligence — is now in the crosshairs of a newly empowered Democratic majority in Washington.

Trading desks are buzzing with fears that federal and state lawmakers will move against the massive, energy-hungry, water-consuming data centres that keep AI running. With key data-centre hubs such as Texas seeing unexpected political shifts, the market is bracing for heavier regulation, constrained access to power and slower corporate growth.

A contagion flying across the globe

“The illusion of endless tech growth shattered today,” warned one senior market strategist, who spoke on condition of anonymity. “People weren’t just taking profits; they were fleeing for the exits. The fear is that the massive capital expenditure poured into AI is about to run straight into a regulatory brick wall.”

The carnage did not stop at the Atlantic. As the closing bell rang in New York, a global contagion took flight. Tokyo’s Nikkei dived roughly 6% in overnight trading, and European markets opened in a sea of red on Friday morning.

President Donald Trump blamed the Democratic election victories for the plunge and urged investors not to panic. Despite the rout, all five major U.S. indexes are still positive for 2026, though the Nasdaq Composite’s year-to-date gain has shrunk from about 10.7% to just 4.6%.

As the weekend approaches, terrified investors are left asking one question: is this a violent post-election knee-jerk reaction, or the beginning of the end for the great AI bull market? One thing is certain — the political landscape has shifted, and Wall Street is bleeding.
