Khawaja defends timing of sandpaper revelations, says Smith “wanted to take the blame”

Usman Khawaja says he has thrown nobody under the bus, revealing that Steve Smith talked him out of going public in 2018 and insisting the whole team should share responsibility.

9 November 2026, 09:00 · Source: ABC News, Outlook India, WION

Former Australia opener Usman Khawaja has defended the timing of his explosive claims about the 2018 ball-tampering scandal, insisting he never meant to blame his former teammates.

In a two-part Instagram explanation posted on Sunday, Khawaja said he was speaking up for Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft, the trio suspended after the Cape Town Test. He argued they were left to carry blame that belonged to the entire team.

Khawaja said he first wanted to come clean in the days after the scandal broke, but Smith, then suspended, persuaded him not to. “He wanted to take the blame,” Khawaja said, adding that the two argued about it.

Claims from his book

The revelations come ahead of the release of Khawaja’s autobiography, Sabr. He has said “all of us” knew about the plan and has suggested sandpaper was also used in the previous Test in Port Elizabeth.

That contradicts Cricket Australia’s findings, which concluded that only Smith, Warner and Bancroft knew of the plot. Smith and Warner were banned for 12 months and Bancroft for nine, and Cricket Australia has since released a statement backing its original investigation.

“Take the blame together”

Khawaja said he is still looking for the same thing he wanted eight years ago: for the whole team, himself included, to accept blame together so everyone can move on. He also said a copy of the relevant chapter was sent to Bancroft beforehand so he would not be blindsided.
