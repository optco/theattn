Beyond Rolex and Omega: Seven Swiss Watch Brands Worth Knowing

Mido, Squale, Traser, Nivada Grenchen, Zodiac, Eterna and Vulcain offer real Swiss watchmaking heritage without the big-name price tag.

23 November 2026, 10:00 · Source: TheAttn

When people think of Swiss watches, the same few names come up: Rolex, Omega, Patek Philippe. Yet Switzerland is home to dozens of lesser-known makers with deep horological pedigrees, distinct technical innovations, and far friendlier price tags. Because they spend less on mass-market celebrity advertising than peers like TAG Heuer or Tudor, watch enthusiasts frequently target them for authentic history and high-spec movements. Here are seven worth a look.

Mido. Founded in 1918 in Solothurn and now backed by the Swatch Group, Mido is known for versatile everyday, dress, and dive watches. The brand pioneered the 1934 Aquadura cork crown seal for early water resistance. Today, its modern architectural polish and Powermatic 80 movement—delivering roughly 80 hours of power reserve—make lines like the Ocean Star, Multifort, and Baroncelli standout values.

Squale. Founded in Neuchâtel in the mid-twentieth century and now family-owned, Squale built its name on dedicated professional dive watches. Before becoming a standalone household name among collectors, it was a legendary case maker that supplied water-resistant cases to heavyweights like Blancpain, Doxa, and Heuer. Its signature 1521 (50 Atmos) remains prized for its chunky, mid-century tool-watch character.

Traser. Built for pure night-vision utility, Traser watches are engineered for tactical, military, and outdoor wearers who need to read the time in total darkness. Owned by MB-Microtec—the inventor of self-powered tritium gas tube illumination (trigalight) supplied across the watch industry—models like the P67 Officer Pro and P99 Q Tactical glow constantly for years without needing an external charge.

Nivada Grenchen. Founded in Grenchen in 1926 and revived in recent years, Nivada Grenchen specialises in faithful 1950s–1970s tool-watch reissues. It is best known for the Antarctic, worn during Operation Deep Freeze in 1955, alongside the rugged Depthmaster dive watch and the Chronomaster Aviator Sea Diver chronograph.

Zodiac. Founded in Le Locle in 1882, Zodiac cemented its place in history in 1953 by launching the Sea Wolf alongside the Blancpain Fifty Fathoms as one of the earliest modern dive watches. Bold mid-century colour palettes and retro styling continue to define its popular Super Sea Wolf dive and GMT collections.

Eterna. Dating back to 1856, Eterna is directly responsible for foundational tech used across Swiss watchmaking: it founded ETA (the industry's largest movement manufacturer) and invented the ball-bearing rotor in 1948 that modernised automatic watches. Today, the KonTiki and Eternamatic lines remain its signature offerings.

Vulcain. Established in Le Locle in 1858, Vulcain created the 1947 "Cricket," the first practical mechanical alarm wristwatch—a timepiece famously worn by multiple U.S. presidents. Alongside its respected heritage alarm watches, the brand has also found renewed acclaim with vintage-inspired sport models like the Skindiver Nautique.

What Sets Them Apart

  • Foundational Industry Pedigree: Several of these brands built the technology behind mainstream luxury watches—from Eterna birthing ETA and the ball-bearing rotor, to Squale engineering cases for top divers, and Traser's parent company supplying tritium tubes to competitors.
  • High Collector Value: With lower marketing overhead, these makers frequently offer COSC-certified calibers, in-house alarm complications, and genuine historical continuity at accessible price points.

For buyers tired of seeing the same big brands on every wrist, these seven prove that Swiss watchmaking runs much deeper than the headline names. Prices, availability, and current ownership vary by brand, so check an authorised retailer before buying.
