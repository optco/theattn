Beyond Rolex and Omega: Seven Swiss Watch Brands Worth Knowing

Mido, Squale, Traser, Nivada Grenchen, Zodiac, Eterna and Vulcain offer real Swiss watchmaking heritage without the big-name price tag.

23 November 2026, 10:00 · Source: TheAttn

When people think of Swiss watches, the same few names come up: Rolex, Omega, Patek Philippe. But Switzerland has dozens of lesser-known makers with long histories, strong designs and, usually, friendlier prices. Here are seven worth a look.

Mido. Founded in 1918 in Solothurn and now part of the Swatch Group, Mido is known for dependable automatics. Its Powermatic 80 movement gives about 80 hours of power reserve, and collections such as Ocean Star and Multifort offer a lot of watch for the money.

Squale. Based in Neuchâtel and founded in 1951, Squale built its name on serious dive watches. The 1521 and the 50 Atmos are its best-known models, loved by collectors for their chunky, tool-watch character.

Traser. Traser watches are made for people who need to read the time in the dark. They use tiny tritium gas tubes that glow constantly for years with no charging, which is why the brand is popular with outdoor users and tactical wearers.

Nivada Grenchen. A heritage name from Grenchen that was revived in recent years. The Antarctic, linked to polar exploration in the late 1940s, and the Depthmaster dive watch have both returned as modern reissues.

Zodiac. Founded in Le Locle in 1882, Zodiac is remembered for the Sea Wolf, launched in 1953 and considered one of the earliest modern dive watches. Its colourful dials and retro styling still define the brand.

Eterna. Dating back to 1856, Eterna is one of the older names in the industry and is credited with popularising the ball-bearing rotor in automatic watches. It still makes its own movements, and the KonTiki line remains its signature.

Vulcain. Established in Le Locle in 1858, Vulcain is best known for the Cricket, a wristwatch with an alarm that was famously worn by several US presidents. It remains a niche but respected choice for fans of mechanical alarm watches.

For buyers tired of seeing the same big brands on every wrist, these seven show that Swiss watchmaking runs much deeper than the headline names. Prices, availability and current ownership vary by brand, so check an authorised retailer before buying.
